Five things to take from this

  1. Look at hospital cancellations before patient behaviour. Hospitals cancelled 13.9m appointments, 1.7 times the 8.2m that patients missed, and the hospital cancellation rate rose from 6.8% in 2015-16 to 9.3% while the DNA rate fell from 6.6% to 5.4%. London cancels 13.9% of appointments; the South West 5.6%. Part of that gap may be recording practice, which is itself worth checking.
  2. Non-attendance is concentrated, so support can be targeted without blaming anyone. Men aged 20-24 miss 13.8% of booked appointments against 3.5% for people aged 75-79. Black patients' DNA rate is 8.8%, 1.81 times the White rate (4.9%). These are the groups to involve in redesigning reminders, booking and timing, not to penalise.
  3. Do not read the ethnicity gap as a fact about ethnicity yet. In the 2021 Census the median age was 43 for the White group, 32 for the Asian and Black groups and 19 for the Mixed group (England and Wales). DNA rates fall steeply with age, so some of the gap is an age effect that these published tables cannot remove. And 18.9% of attendances carry no ethnicity at all. Better recording is a precondition for monitoring inequality, not an afterthought.
  4. Remote appointments are missed less often, but that is not proof they cause better attendance. First appointments by telephone or video have a recorded DNA rate of 4.3% against 7.0% in person. Patients, specialties and the way a missed call is recorded all differ between the two. A remote-first change should be piloted with an evaluation plan and with in-person routes kept open.
  5. Most variation between trusts is not a performance signal. On plain statistical limits 142 of 151 trusts look like outliers; after allowing for real between-trust differences, 4 sit above and 2 below the 99.8% limits. The high ones are mental health and community trusts and a children's hospital, whose appointments are not like an acute hospital's; the two lowest are community trusts with DNA rates under 1%, which points to how DNAs are recorded. The next step is to compare like with like, by trust type.

Ethnicity: a real gap, measured on incomplete data

With 97 million appointments in the White group alone, the 95% intervals are a few hundredths of a percentage point wide, so chance is not the issue. Age structure and missing ethnicity are. Under NHS England's Core20PLUS5 approach, local systems define their own PLUS groups, and ethnic minority communities are among those NHS England expects to see identified. This is the kind of evidence a local team would bring to that work, after age-standardising with local data. Census medians from ONS, Ethnic group by age and sex, England and Wales: Census 2021.

Regions differ, and London stands out on cancellations

The most deprived areas have the lowest first-attendance rate

The most deprived tenth of areas has 4.9% fewer first attendances per head than the least deprived tenth. Need for care is higher in deprived areas, but their populations are also younger, so a crude rate cannot say whether access is worse. It is a question to test with age- and need-adjusted local data, not a finding.

Trusts are identified by joining HES provider codes to the ODS register of NHS trusts (etr). 14 large trusts appear in HES with a -X suffix that the HES metadata does not explain; they match ODS once the suffix is removed. 235 other providers with 10,000+ appointments are independent-sector organisations (or a closed trust code) and are not on this chart.

In general practice, mode matters even more

In 2026-09, 60% of GP appointments were face to face and 12% video or online. "Online" includes asynchronous online consultations, where there is little to miss, so the near-zero rate is partly a property of the mode itself.

Data quality and reconciliation

  • HES headline rebuilt exactly from the provider file: 150,317,821 appointments, 8,151,086 DNAs.
  • GP September 2026: the publication's headline is 35.1 million appointments "recorded across General Practice"; the National Overview file covers the 34.1 million in GP appointment books from 98.9% of practices. This file sums to 34,134,084. The first version of this project typed in the 35.1m headline next to percentages from a narrower base.
  • Every regional and provider count in the HES provider file is a multiple of 5 and small counts appear as *; national figures are exact. Suppressed cells are counted as zero in provider totals and the number affected is recorded per trust.
  • 18.9% of attendances have ethnicity 'Not known' or 'Not stated'.
  • HES assigns deprivation deciles with the 2010 Index of Multiple Deprivation.
